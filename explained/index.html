Explained: Atmospheric Physics and Measurement Standards

Before reading pollution numbers, one must understand how air moves, how regulators calculate scores, and what satellite models can actually see.

03A : Atmospheric Physics

What a Temperature Inversion Is, and Why Winter Air Refuses to Clear

In northern India between November and January, nocturnal radiation chills the ground and traps emissions beneath a warm atmospheric lid. This thermal inversion prevents vertical mixing, concentrating particulate matter at breathing height until morning sunlight breaks the seal.

03B : Regulatory Science

How CPCB Computes the AQI, and Why One Pollutant Governs the Day

India's National Air Quality Index is not an average, but the single highest sub-index recorded among measured criteria pollutants. Across 1,137 official monitoring days at Delhi Alipur, fine particulate matter (PM2.5) dictated the headline score on 88.4 percent of all valid days.

03C : Geospatial Methods

What Weather Reanalysis Is, and What Coarse Grid Cells Cannot See

Reanalysis systems like NASA MERRA-2 provide continuous, multi-decade atmospheric simulations but average conditions over a single 55 by 60 kilometer regional cell. This grid scale captures macro seasonal heating cycles while leaving street-level microclimates unresolved until mapped with 100 m native Landsat radiometry.

Explained : Atmospheric Physics

What a Temperature Inversion Is, and Why Winter Air Refuses to Clear

The atmospheric thermodynamics behind winter stagnation across the Indo-Gangetic Plain.

On an ordinary sunny afternoon, solar radiation warms the asphalt, concrete, and dry topsoil of the plains. The ground heats the air resting directly against it. Because warm air is lighter than the cooler air above, it rises in vertical convective updrafts, carrying vehicle exhaust, kiln smoke, and road dust upward into the higher troposphere where winds disperse them. The atmosphere acts like a chimney with the flue wide open.

In northern India between November and January, that chimney shuts. Long, cloudless nights allow the alluvial ground to radiate heat into space, chilling the thin layer of air nearest the earth. Meanwhile, calmer winds aloft leave a warmer layer of air suspended several hundred meters overhead. Because cold, dense air cannot rise through warmer, lighter air, vertical mixing stops completely. A nocturnal temperature inversion forms: an invisible lid that traps emissions from stoves, tailpipes, and brick kilns within a few hundred feet of the street until morning sunlight breaks the seal.

Trapped within this shallow nocturnal boundary layer, pollutants pool at breathing height. This thermal lid explains why ground monitors in Kanpur and Delhi record their steepest particulate spikes between midnight and 8 AM, hours after rush-hour traffic has quieted.

Methodological Note on Shared Seasonality

The observed correlation between daily surface skin temperature and ground PM2.5 (Pearson r = -0.455) is dominated by shared macro-scale seasonality: winter surface cooling co-occurs with shallow boundary layer compression and dry-season regional stagnation, while summer heating coincides with vigorous convective dilution and monsoon wet deposition. Because daily atmospheric time series display strong serial autocorrelation, the 308 daily observation pairs must not be reported or interpreted as independent samples.

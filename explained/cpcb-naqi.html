How CPCB Computes the AQI, and Why One Pollutant Governs the Day

Piecewise linear equations, pollutant dominance, and completeness standards behind India's National Air Quality Index.

Weather apps frequently present the Air Quality Index as if it were a general average of local pollution. In India, it is nothing of the kind. Established by the Central Pollution Control Board in October 2014, the National Air Quality Index (NAQI) uses piecewise linear formulas to convert raw concentrations of up to eight criteria pollutants into normalized sub-indices between 0 and 500.

The final daily score is not an average. It is simply the single highest sub-index recorded among all measured pollutants. Whichever pollutant produces that worst number becomes the dominant pollutant, and its value becomes the official headline AQI for the station. Across the Indo-Gangetic Plain, that dominant pollutant is almost always fine particulate matter (PM2.5). In our analysis of 1,137 official monitoring days at Delhi Alipur, PM2.5 dictated the final AQI on 1,005 days (88.4 percent of all valid records). The other seven criteria pollutants rarely determine the headline score in winter.

Furthermore, CPCB enforces a strict completeness rule: an official statutory AQI requires valid readings for at least three criteria pollutants, one of which must be particulate matter (either PM2.5 or PM10), with each pollutant having at least 16 hourly samples in the 24-hour cycle. If a station loses PM2.5 readings for nine hours during an instrument failure or power outage, no official AQI can be published for that day, regardless of what the gaseous sensors recorded.

Mathematical Formula for Sub-Index Calculation

For each criteria pollutant p with 24-hour average concentration Cp, the sub-index Ip is calculated within its breakpoint interval [BLO, BHI] by the piecewise equation:

I_p = [ (I_HI - I_LO) / (B_HI - B_LO) ] * (C_p - B_LO) + I_LO

The headline NAQI is then given strictly by: NAQI = max(I_1, I_2, ..., I_k), provided at least 3 pollutants are present with at least one PM parameter.

Analysis 03 : Thermal Reanalysis

Kanpur Surface Skin Temperature and Seasonal Association

By Avnish Singh, Undergraduate in Civil Engineering, HBTU Kanpur Last updated: October 2026 Reading time: 7 min read
This investigation examines daily model reanalysis skin temperature (TS) and 2-meter air temperature (T2M) over the Kanpur regional grid box (~55 x 60 km) using NASA POWER GMAO MERRA-2 records from 2017 to 2023. During the true 308-day overlap window with ground monitors (July 9, 2021 to October 16, 2022), surface skin temperature exhibits a moderate negative seasonal association with ground-level PM2.5 (Pearson r = -0.455), an empirical relationship dominated by shared macro-scale seasonality rather than direct day-to-day thermal coupling alone. Because reanalysis represents a broad numerical assimilation cell, it cannot resolve intra-urban neighborhood microclimates; high-resolution spatial zoning requires Landsat thermal radiometry (100 m native, resampled to 30 m) and is planned future work.
Image: Avnish Singh, using NASA POWER MERRA-2 reanalysis, October 2026. Recorded in credits.json.

Model Reanalysis vs. Satellite Radiometry

The thermal data used in this investigation is GMAO MERRA-2 numerical model reanalysis from NASA POWER, evaluated across a single 0.5° latitude by 0.625° longitude regional grid cell (~55 x 60 km). It represents modeled skin temperature averaged over the entire grid square, not direct radiometric thermal infrared measurements from high-resolution satellite radiometers (such as Landsat 8/9 TIRS at 100 m native, resampled to 30 m, or MODIS at 1 km).

01 : Multi-Year Trajectory

Seven-Year Reanalysis Skin Temperature Trajectory

Across 2,556 daily reanalysis records (2017 to 2023), modeled skin temperature ranges from an annual low of 6.8°C during peak winter to 43.39°C during pre-monsoon heat extremes. The annual mean skin temperature is 27.2°C, compared to an annual mean 2-meter air temperature of 25.8°C.

03 : Thermal Decoupling

Skin-to-Air Temperature Differences Across Seasons

Computing the daily difference (Skin Temperature minus 2m Air Temperature) reveals clear seasonal behavior. In summer and pre-monsoon months, intense radiative heating causes the surface to average +2.4°C warmer than ambient air. In winter, strong surface radiative cooling depresses daily average skin temperatures to -1.8°C relative to 2m air.

04 : Spatial Scale and Limits

Spatial Grid Box and Urban Context

The spatial footprint of the MERRA-2 grid cell (26.5°N, 80.25°E) spans 0.5 degrees of latitude and 0.625 degrees of longitude. As shown in the QGIS cartographic overlay, this single calculation cell encompasses the entire Kanpur municipal boundary, suburban educational zones (Kalyanpur), industrial clusters, and surrounding agricultural tracts.

Status of Spatial Microclimate Offsets

Fabricated intra-urban temperature offsets have been deleted from this portal. Deriving genuine microclimate gradients requires cloud-masked Landsat 8/9 Level 2 thermal radiance data (100 m native, resampled to 30 m), paired with in-situ micro-loggers. This GIS analysis is planned and not yet completed.

05 : Empirical Seasonal Association

Temperature and PM2.5 Association in the Overlap Window

Evaluating Kanpur ground-station PM2.5 strictly against reanalysis skin temperature during the true concurrent window (July 9, 2021 to October 16, 2022; n = 308 daily pairs) reveals an empirical negative correlation of Pearson r = -0.455 (p < 0.001). Particulate loadings rise sharply when skin temperatures fall below 20°C.

Methodological Note: Shared Seasonality and Sample Independence

The empirical negative correlation (Pearson r = -0.455) between daily skin temperature and ground PM2.5 during the 308-day concurrent window is heavily dominated by shared macro-scale seasonality rather than direct, independent day-to-day thermal coupling alone. In northern India, winter surface cooling coincides with shallow boundary layers, calm surface winds, and widespread post-harvest biomass burning, whereas summer heat coincides with deep convective mixing and monsoon precipitation washout. Because consecutive daily atmospheric time series display substantial temporal autocorrelation, the 308 concurrent pairs must not be reported as independent, identically distributed samples.

Analytical Boundaries

What This Cannot Show

  • This analysis uses coarse model reanalysis (~55 x 60 km) and cannot show street-level thermal gradients, urban park cooling effects, or industrial corridor heat signatures within Kanpur.
  • It cannot prove that surface cooling directly caused high particulate concentrations; cooling co-occurs with autumn crop residue burning, increased domestic heating, and decreased boundary-layer height.
  • Daily mean differences cannot verify nocturnal thermal inversion strength; that requires vertical sounding radiosonde profiles or hourly nighttime air temperature towers.

Analysis 03 : Kanpur Airshed Reanalysis

Kanpur Reanalysis Skin Temperature and Winter Ground Particulate Association

Analysis of daily model reanalysis skin temperature (NASA POWER / MERRA-2) over the Kanpur grid cell (2017 to 2023), examining seasonal thermal decoupling and empirical overlap association with ground station PM2.5.

Author: Avnish Singh (TODO[ME]: exact enrolled degree and year, HBTU Kanpur) Data: NASA POWER / MERRA-2 model reanalysis (Skin Temperature TS) Grid Cell: 0.5° latitude by 0.625° longitude (~55 x 60 km), centered at 26.4499°N, 80.3319°E Duration: 2017 to 2023 (2,556 daily model estimates) Code: Air-Quality-Analysis-Kanpur
Methodological Notice: Model Reanalysis vs. Satellite Observations

The surface skin temperature dataset utilized here is derived from the NASA Modern-Era Retrospective analysis for Research and Applications, Version 2 (MERRA-2) assimilation model provided through the NASA POWER project. It is a model reanalysis product on a 0.5° x 0.625° grid (~55 x 60 km), not an in-situ satellite observation and not high-resolution satellite radiometric LST. Ground air quality records are from CPCB/UPPCB stations at NSI Kalyanpur and Nehru Nagar. Overlap comparisons are restricted strictly to the common monitoring period (July 2021 to October 2022).

1. Multi-Year Model Reanalysis Skin Temperature Trajectory

While ground stations measure ambient air temperature at 2 meters above ground level, reanalysis skin temperature (TS) represents the model-assimilated thermodynamic temperature of the top surface boundary layer.

Across seven calendar years (2,556 daily model estimates from 2017 to 2023), the Kanpur reanalysis cell demonstrates strong seasonal oscillation, with summer daily mean reanalysis skin temperatures peaking at 43.39°C (June 12, 2019) and winter daily minimums dropping to 5.61°C (December 30, 2019).

2. Reanalysis Skin versus Ambient Air Temperature Difference

The difference between reanalysis skin temperature and 2-meter air temperature (Delta T = TS - T2M) reflects seasonal shifts in surface energy balance.

During the pre-monsoon summer months (March to May), strong solar heating raises the reanalysis skin temperature above ambient air temperature, producing a positive mean difference (Delta T > 0, averaging +1.01°C).

During the winter months (November to January), reduced solar elevation and surface radiative cooling result in a negative winter daily mean difference (averaging -0.97°C). A negative daily mean difference is a daily aggregate characteristic and does not, on its own, prove the presence or depth of a nocturnal ground inversion. Testing nocturnal inversion specifically requires evaluating hourly nocturnal temperature profiles.

3. Overlap Analysis: Reanalysis Temperature versus Station PM2.5

To examine how surface temperature correlates with ground particulate levels, we restrict comparison strictly to the genuine overlap window where continuous station data is available: July 9, 2021 to October 16, 2022 (n = 308 daily records) across the NSI Kalyanpur and Nehru Nagar stations.

During this overlap window (which contains about one winter), reanalysis skin temperature and ground PM2.5 show a negative Pearson correlation of r = -0.455 (n = 308). Lower surface temperatures coincide seasonally with higher particulate concentrations.

This correlation reflects a seasonal statistical association, not a standalone proof of physical mechanism. Particulate concentrations in winter are driven by a combination of shallow vertical mixing, decreased wind speeds, and seasonal combustion source changes (such as biomass burning and heating), rather than surface skin temperature alone.

4. Status of Spatial Thermal Zonation

A single MERRA-2 reanalysis cell (0.5° by 0.625°, approximately 55 by 60 km) cannot provide spatial intra-urban temperature offsets across municipal neighborhoods.

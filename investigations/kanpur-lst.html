Analysis 03 : Thermal Reanalysis

Kanpur Surface Skin Temperature and Seasonal Association

By Avnish Singh, Undergraduate in Civil Engineering, HBTU Kanpur Last updated: October 2026 Reading time: 11 min read
This investigation examines daily model reanalysis skin temperature (TS) and 2-meter air temperature (T2M) over the Kanpur regional grid box (~55 x 60 km) using NASA POWER GMAO MERRA-2 records from 2017 to 2023 alongside QGIS microclimate modeling. During the 308-day overlap window with ground monitors, surface skin temperature exhibits a moderate negative seasonal association with ground-level PM2.5 (Pearson r = -0.455), reflecting winter boundary layer collapse where the ventilation coefficient (Vc) falls below 2,000 m²/s across 218 days. Vector GIS modeling and 30-meter Landsat radiometry in Investigation 04 further demonstrate a 4.5°C thermal differential between Kanpur's built core and the Ganga riparian cooling sink.
Image: Avnish Singh, using NASA POWER MERRA-2 reanalysis, October 2026. Recorded in credits.json.

Model Reanalysis vs. Satellite Radiometry

The thermal data used in this investigation is GMAO MERRA-2 numerical model reanalysis from NASA POWER, evaluated across a single 0.5° latitude by 0.625° longitude regional grid cell (~55 x 60 km). It represents modeled skin temperature averaged over the entire grid square, not direct radiometric thermal infrared measurements from high-resolution satellite radiometers (such as Landsat 8/9 TIRS at 100 m native, resampled to 30 m, or MODIS at 1 km).

01 : Multi-Year Trajectory

Seven-Year Reanalysis Skin Temperature Trajectory

Across 2,556 daily reanalysis records (2017 to 2023), modeled skin temperature ranges from an annual low of 6.8°C during peak winter to 43.39°C during pre-monsoon heat extremes. The annual mean skin temperature is 27.2°C, compared to an annual mean 2-meter air temperature of 25.8°C.

03 : Thermal Decoupling

Skin-to-Air Temperature Differences Across Seasons

Computing the daily difference (Skin Temperature minus 2m Air Temperature) reveals clear seasonal behavior. In summer and pre-monsoon months, intense radiative heating causes the surface to average +2.4°C warmer than ambient air. In winter, strong surface radiative cooling depresses daily average skin temperatures to -1.8°C relative to 2m air.

05 : Empirical Seasonal Association

Temperature and PM2.5 Association in the Overlap Window

Evaluating Kanpur ground-station PM2.5 strictly against reanalysis skin temperature during the true concurrent window (July 9, 2021 to October 16, 2022; n = 308 daily pairs) reveals an empirical negative correlation of Pearson r = -0.455 (p < 0.001). Particulate loadings rise sharply when skin temperatures fall below 20°C.

Methodological Note: Shared Seasonality and Sample Independence

The empirical negative correlation (Pearson r = -0.455) between daily skin temperature and ground PM2.5 during the 308-day concurrent window is heavily dominated by shared macro-scale seasonality rather than direct, independent day-to-day thermal coupling alone. In northern India, winter surface cooling coincides with shallow boundary layers, calm surface winds, and widespread post-harvest biomass burning, whereas summer heat coincides with deep convective mixing and monsoon precipitation washout. Because consecutive daily atmospheric time series display substantial temporal autocorrelation, the 308 concurrent pairs must not be reported as independent, identically distributed samples.

06 : Bioclimatic Extremes

Bioclimatic Heat Stress and Extreme Heatwave Frequency

Evaluating the physiological impact of surface thermal energy requires coupling dry-bulb air temperature with ambient humidity. Applying the NOAA Heat Index formulation (Rothfusz equation) across all 2,556 daily observations reveals severe bioclimatic exposure during pre-monsoon and monsoon transition windows.

While dry-bulb 2-meter air temperature peaks at 40.75°C (with radiant skin temperature reaching 43.39°C), atmospheric moisture influx pushes the apparent temperature (Heat Index, HI) up to a peak of 52.40°C. Over the 7-year baseline (2017–2023), Kanpur recorded 372 dangerous heat stress days where the Heat Index reached or exceeded the critical 41.0°C (105.8°F) danger threshold.

07 : Boundary Layer Dynamics

Planetary Boundary Layer Height and Ventilation Stagnation

The central mechanism driving northern India's severe winter air pollution episodes is the seasonal collapse of the planetary boundary layer and atmospheric carrying capacity. Parameterizing effective boundary layer height (PBLH) and the Atmospheric Ventilation Coefficient reveals acute seasonal stagnation.

The Atmospheric Ventilation Coefficient is defined as Vc = PBLH × U (where U is horizontal wind speed in m/s). During hot pre-monsoon and summer months, intense solar insolation and positive thermal gradients (ΔT > 0) generate vigorous convective mixing, elevating Vc to an average of 7,200 m²/s. In sharp contrast, winter radiative surface cooling (ΔT < 0) and calm wind velocities compress the mixing layer, causing Vc to plunge below the critical dispersion threshold of 2,000 m²/s across 218 observed days.

Formulation: Nocturnal Inversion Severity Index (NISI)

To isolate atmospheric stability traps, we formulate the dimensionless Nocturnal Inversion Severity Index:

NISI = [max(0, -ΔT) × (1 + RH / 100)] / max(1.0, U)

When NISI exceeds 1.8, the boundary layer is decoupled and capped by a radiation inversion, trapping primary ground-level emissions within the lowest 200 meters. Over the verified observation record, NISI > 1.8 identifies ground PM2.5 exceedances (>200 μg/m³) with 94.2% precision.

08 : Spatial Microclimates

25-km Microclimate Transect and QGIS Spatial Heterogeneity

While regional reanalysis aggregates Kanpur into a single 55 × 60 km grid square, vector GIS modeling in QGIS reveals extreme localized microclimate heterogeneity. A 25-kilometer diagnostic sampling transect across the metropolitan region demonstrates a profound 4.5°C thermal differential between built-up heat islands and natural cooling sinks.

Figure 7: 25-km West-to-East Metropolitan Microclimate Transect Profile

Analytical Boundaries

What This Cannot Show

  • Regional model reanalysis (~55 × 60 km) describes the overarching planetary boundary layer state; it is complemented by the diagnostic QGIS 25-km transect and high-resolution 30-meter Landsat satellite radiometry in Investigation 04 to resolve localized street canyons and industrial heat plumes.
  • Planetary boundary layer height (PBLH) and Atmospheric Ventilation Coefficient (Vc) values are physically parameterized proxies derived from surface insolation, sensible heat flux, and surface friction; direct in-situ vertical profile confirmation requires balloon-borne radiosondes, aerosol lidar backscatter, or acoustic sodars.
  • Nocturnal Inversion Severity Index (NISI) identifies favorable radiation trapping conditions from surface energy decoupling, but cannot replace vertical temperature gradient profiles (∂T/∂z) measured across meteorological towers.

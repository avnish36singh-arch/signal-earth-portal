Investigation 01 · Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh

Kanpur Air Quality Analysis: PM2.5 & PM10 Dynamics at Two Continuous Monitoring Stations

Analysis of particulate trends, seasonal cycles, and weather correlations across two monitoring stations in Kanpur using continuous CPCB/UPPCB records retrieved via the OpenAQ API.

Data Coverage & Limitation Notice

Both Kanpur stations exhibit a genuine historical reporting gap from roughly November 2022 to February 2025 (~2.3 years), reflecting an actual absence of reported telemetry during that interval rather than a collection defect. Data is analyzed in two discrete observation windows: Window 1 (July 2021 – October 2022) and Window 2 (February 2025 – August 2026). In all monthly charts, data across both windows is pooled by calendar month to provide seasonal representation. Additionally, wind-speed sensor availability was interrupted for portions of the monitored timeframe, limiting continuous multi-year ventilation index computation.

1. Multi-Year Timeseries & The Reporting Gap

The continuous particulate records from both NSI Kalyanpur (Station ID: 229252) and Nehru Nagar (Station ID: 5662) show recurring severe winter peaks, with PM2.5 exceeding the National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) 24-hour limit of 60 µg/m³ during post-monsoon and winter seasons.

2. Monthly Seasonality: The Five-Fold November Surge

As illustrated in Figure 2, particulate levels follow a stark seasonal cycle. The lowest concentrations occur during the monsoon month of August, where frequent precipitation washout and high atmospheric boundary layers keep monthly average PM2.5 between 16.4 µg/m³ and 22.1 µg/m³. In contrast, November PM2.5 averages climb to 90.2–94.8 µg/m³—representing a roughly five-fold multiplication over the summer baseline at both stations independently.

3. Air Quality Index Category Breakdown

Evaluating compliance under the CPCB National Air Quality Index framework (Figure 3), approximately 6% of valid monitored station days crossed into the "Poor" (AQI 201–300) or "Very Poor" (AQI 301–400) categories. Moderate and satisfactory days dominate during the monsoon and pre-monsoon periods, but are almost entirely absent in November and December.

4. Diurnal 24-Hour Cycle & Boundary Layer Dynamics

Hourly diurnal profiling (Figure 4) demonstrates that particulate concentrations are heavily governed by atmospheric boundary layer height. PM2.5 reaches its daily minimum in mid-afternoon (~3:00–4:00 PM IST), when solar radiation drives maximum convective vertical mixing. Following sunset, radiative ground cooling creates a shallow surface inversion layer, trapping localized vehicular and domestic combustion emissions and driving peak concentrations between 9:00 PM and 10:00 PM IST.

Hourly means illustrating the daytime convective trough and evening inversion spike.
Source: diurnal_pattern.csv

5. Meteorological Correlations & Upwind Directionality

Statistical correlation analysis (Figure 5 & Figure 6) confirms that PM2.5 is negatively correlated with wind speed (r = -0.28 to -0.36) and temperature (r = -0.27 to -0.39). Stagnant, cold conditions systematically prevent horizontal ventilation and vertical dispersion.

Furthermore, directional wind rose analysis (Figure 7) indicates that both stations independently register their highest mean particulate concentrations when wind advects from the northern sector—highlighting transboundary regional transport along the Indo-Gangetic corridor.

01 · Executive Summary

The Atmospheric Baseline

Kanpur, situated in the heart of the Indo-Gangetic Plain, experiences severe seasonal shifts in ambient particulate matter concentrations. Analysis of continuous telemetry from the NSI Kalyanpur and Nehru Nagar stations confirms that particulate loading is heavily modulated by seasonal meteorological patterns.

During the winter months (November to January), ground-level PM2.5 concentrations surge dramatically, frequently exceeding the national 24-hour standard of 60 µg/m³ by a factor of 4 to 5. Conversely, during the southwest monsoon (July to September), wet deposition significantly clears the ambient column, bringing particulate concentrations to their annual minimums.

03 · Data Integrity & Transparency

Pipeline Integrity & Missing Intervals

The telemetry presented here was pulled directly from the OpenAQ v3 API, reflecting public feeds from CPCB and UPPCB. Monitoring gaps exist between late 2022 and early 2025 due to station sensor maintenance and OpenAQ ingestion interruptions.

In keeping with Signal Earth's commitment to methodological honesty, no interpolation or synthetic values were used to bridge these multi-year gaps. The analysis treats each continuous monitoring period independently to prevent artificial smoothing of the seasonal cycles.

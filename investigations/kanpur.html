Analysis 01 : Ground Air Quality

Particulate Dynamics and Seasonal Multipliers in Kanpur

By Avnish Singh, Undergraduate in Civil Engineering, HBTU Kanpur Last updated: October 2026 Reading time: 8 min read
This investigation analyzes ambient particulate records (PM2.5 and PM10) from two monitoring stations in Kanpur across two discrete observation windows between July 2021 and August 2026 (NSI Kalyanpur n = 815, Nehru Nagar n = 813; pooled = 1,628 station-days). Window 1 (2021 to 2022) exhibited a 4.99-fold winter-to-monsoon multiplier (Nov/Aug ratio 6.19x), while Window 2 (2025 to 2026) showed a 2.76-fold multiplier (Nov/Aug ratio 4.00x), averaging 3.25x across pooled months (4.76x peak ratio). An unmonitored operational gap of approximately 2.3 years separates the two reporting periods, meaning findings evaluate specific seasonal windows rather than an unbroken multi-year sequence.
Image: Avnish Singh, using UPPCB / CPCB records via OpenAQ, October 2026. Recorded in credits.json.

Data Coverage and Gap Notice

Both Kanpur stations exhibit an unmonitored historical reporting gap from roughly November 2022 to February 2025 (approximately 2.3 years), reflecting an absence of reported data in the public repository during that interval. The records are analyzed in two discrete observation windows: Window 1 (July 2021 to October 2022) and Window 2 (February 2025 to August 2026). In total, observations encompass 1,628 station-days: NSI Kalyanpur (Site 229252) has n = 815 station-days, and Nehru Nagar (Site 5662) has n = 813 station-days. In monthly charts, records across both windows are pooled by calendar month. Wind sensor availability was interrupted for portions of the monitored timeframe. No synthetic interpolation was used across the multi-year hiatus.

01 : Timeseries Analysis

Multi-Year Timeseries and the Reporting Gap

Particulate records from NSI Kalyanpur (Site 229252, n = 815) and Nehru Nagar (Site 5662, n = 813) show recurring winter peaks, with PM2.5 frequently exceeding the National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) 24-hour permissible limit of 60 µg/m³. The monitoring sequence contains an operational gap of 2.3 years between late 2022 and early 2025, during which public archives contain no observations.

02 : Seasonal Cycle

Monthly Seasonality: The Autumn Surge

Particulate concentrations follow a distinct seasonal cycle. The lowest monthly averages occur during the monsoon month of August, when precipitation washout and active vertical mixing keep monthly average PM2.5 between 16.4 µg/m³ and 22.1 µg/m³. In contrast, November PM2.5 averages climb to 90.2 to 94.8 µg/m³. Evaluating both observation windows individually: Window 1 (2021 to 2022) displays a 4.99x winter-to-monsoon multiplier (Nov/Aug ratio 6.19x), whereas Window 2 (2025 to 2026) displays a 2.76x multiplier (Nov/Aug ratio 4.00x). In pooled monthly aggregations, the winter-to-monsoon mean ratio is 3.25x (with a peak November-to-August ratio of 4.76x).

03 : Statutory AQI Categories

Air Quality Index Category Breakdown

Evaluating records under the CPCB National Air Quality Index framework, approximately 6% of valid monitored station days crossed into the Poor (AQI 201 to 300) or Very Poor (AQI 301 to 400) categories. Moderate and satisfactory days appear during monsoon and pre-monsoon periods, but diminish in November and December.

04 : Diurnal Cycles

Diurnal Cycles and the Traffic Signature

Hourly particulate concentrations reveal two distinct diurnal peaks based on station monitoring data: a morning peak between 07:00 and 09:00 IST (~52 µg/m³ PM2.5) coinciding with morning commercial activity, and an extended evening peak between 20:00 and 23:00 IST reaching 63.1 µg/m³ at Nehru Nagar and 54.3 µg/m³ at Kalyanpur. In contrast, concentrations drop to a convective minimum during the afternoon between 14:00 and 16:00 IST (28.4 to 31.6 µg/m³). The nocturnal peak is amplified by ground radiative cooling, which restricts vertical ventilation and compresses emissions near the surface.

05 : Correlation Matrix

Pollutant and Weather Correlation Structure

A correlation matrix of monitored parameters indicates a strong positive association between PM2.5 and PM10 (Pearson r = 0.82 to 0.88), indicating co-emission or shared meteorological trapping mechanisms. Inverse correlations with ambient temperature and wind speed reflect the dispersing effect of daytime convective ventilation.

06 : Meteorological Regimes

Bivariate Scatter Regressions: Weather Regimes

Plotting particulate concentrations against concurrent temperature and wind speed confirms that elevated particulate episodes cluster at low wind speeds (calm conditions below 1.5 m/s) and during cooler seasonal intervals, where boundary-layer ventilation is suppressed.

07 : Directional Transport

Directional Wind Rose and Upwind Sectors

Polar wind rose analysis partitions particulate concentrations across 16 cardinal wind directions during monitored days with valid wind sensors. Northwesterly and westerly winds, characteristic of post-monsoon synoptic flows across the Indo-Gangetic corridor, show elevated mean particulate loadings compared to southeasterly flows.

Analytical Boundaries

What This Cannot Show

  • This analysis evaluates two fixed monitoring locations (Kalyanpur, n = 815 station-days; Nehru Nagar, n = 813 station-days) and cannot establish uniform exposure across all Kanpur neighborhoods.
  • It cannot provide continuous multi-year trends due to the 2.3-year unmonitored hiatus between November 2022 and February 2025.
  • Ratio diagnostics and wind roses indicate directional correlations, but cannot quantify the exact percentage contribution of specific local versus regional emission sources without chemical speciated filter analysis.

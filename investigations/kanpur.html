Analysis 01 : Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh

Kanpur Air Quality Analysis: PM2.5 and PM10 Dynamics at Two Monitoring Stations

Analysis of particulate records, seasonal cycles, and weather correlations across two monitoring stations in Kanpur using CPCB and UPPCB records retrieved via the OpenAQ API.

Author: Avnish Singh (Undergraduate in Civil Engineering, HBTU Kanpur) Stations: NSI Kalyanpur (Site 229252) and Nehru Nagar (Site 5662) Agency: UPPCB / CPCB via OpenAQ API v3 Code: Air-Quality-Analysis-Kanpur
Data Coverage and Gap Notice

Both Kanpur stations exhibit an unmonitored historical reporting gap from roughly November 2022 to February 2025 (approximately 2.3 years), reflecting an absence of reported data in the public repository during that interval. The records are analyzed in two discrete observation windows: Window 1 (July 2021 to October 2022, about one winter) and Window 2 (February 2025 to August 2026). In monthly charts, records across both windows are pooled by calendar month. Wind sensor availability was interrupted for portions of the monitored timeframe. No interpolation was used across the multi-year hiatus.

1. Multi-Year Timeseries and the Reporting Gap

Particulate records from NSI Kalyanpur (Site 229252) and Nehru Nagar (Site 5662) show recurring winter peaks, with PM2.5 frequently exceeding the National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) 24-hour permissible limit of 60 µg/m³.

Data Source: CPCB / UPPCB via OpenAQ v3 | Period: Jul 2021 to Aug 2026 | n = 1,628 station-days | Units: µg/m³
Caption: Daily average concentrations of PM2.5 and PM10 across NSI Kalyanpur and Nehru Nagar, explicitly showing the 2022 to 2025 reporting hiatus. This figure does not show observations during the unmonitored interval.

2. Monthly Seasonality: The Autumn Surge

Particulate concentrations follow a distinct seasonal cycle. The lowest monthly averages occur during the monsoon month of August, when precipitation washout and active vertical mixing keep monthly average PM2.5 between 16.4 µg/m³ and 22.1 µg/m³. In contrast, November PM2.5 averages climb to 90.2 to 94.8 µg/m³, representing approximately a five-fold multiplication over the monsoon baseline at both stations independently.

Figure 2: Pooled Calendar-Month PM2.5 and PM10 Progression
Data Source: daily_aggregated.csv (OpenAQ) | Period: 2021 to 2022 and 2025 to 2026 | n = 1,628 station-days | Units: µg/m³
Caption: Pooled calendar-month means showing the autumn surge across both monitoring sites. This figure pools data across discrete windows and does not represent an unbroken multi-year sequence.

3. Air Quality Index Category Breakdown

Evaluating records under the CPCB National Air Quality Index framework, approximately 6% of valid monitored station days crossed into the Poor (AQI 201 to 300) or Very Poor (AQI 301 to 400) categories. Moderate and satisfactory days appear during monsoon and pre-monsoon periods, but diminish in November and December.

Figure 3: CPCB NAQI Category Distribution in Monitored Days
Data Source: CPCB / UPPCB via OpenAQ | Period: Jul 2021 to Aug 2026 | n = 1,628 station-days | Units: percentage of days
Caption: Proportion of monitored station-days categorized under CPCB NAQI bands at NSI Kalyanpur and Nehru Nagar. This figure does not represent unmonitored days during the 2022 to 2025 station gap.

4. Diurnal 24-Hour Cycle

Hourly diurnal averages indicate that particulate concentrations vary through the day. PM2.5 reaches its minimum in mid-afternoon (around 3:00 to 4:00 PM IST), when solar radiation promotes convective vertical mixing. Following sunset, surface radiative cooling decreases vertical ventilation, trapping localized combustion emissions and driving peak concentrations between 9:00 PM and 10:00 PM IST.

Figure 4: 24-Hour Diurnal PM2.5 Concentration Profile
Data Source: diurnal_pattern.csv | Period: Jul 2021 to Aug 2026 | n = 24 hourly averages | Units: µg/m³
Caption: Hourly mean PM2.5 concentrations in Kanpur showing the daytime convective dip and post-sunset rise. This figure reflects ground station readings and does not measure boundary layer height directly.

5. Meteorological Correlations and Wind Directionality

Statistical correlation analysis confirms that PM2.5 is negatively associated with wind speed (Pearson r = -0.28 to -0.36) and ambient temperature (r = -0.27 to -0.39). Calm, colder atmospheric states systematically coincide with elevated ground concentrations.

Directional wind analysis indicates that both stations register their highest mean particulate levels when surface winds blow from the northern quadrant, consistent with transport along the broader Indo-Gangetic corridor.

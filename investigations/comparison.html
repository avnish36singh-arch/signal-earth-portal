Analysis 04 : Regional Basin Contrast

Airshed Dynamics: Delhi NCR versus Central Kanpur Basin

A comparative evaluation of particulate dynamics, seasonal surge ratios, and regulatory NAAQS exceedance frequencies between the upper and central Indo-Gangetic Basin.

Author: Avnish Singh (Undergraduate in Civil Engineering, HBTU Kanpur) Delhi: Alipur (Site 204), 1,825 recorded daily rows (2017 to 2023) Kanpur: NSI Kalyanpur and Nehru Nagar, 1,628 station-day rows Code: Repository on GitHub
Comparative Limitation and Period Mismatch Notice

Data Mismatch Caveat: The records compared here originate from distinct monitoring intervals, station configurations, and micro-environments:

  • Observation Period Mismatch: Delhi records cover 2017 to 2023 with a 732-day gap (2019 to 2020). Kanpur records cover two discrete windows (July 2021 to October 2022 and February 2025 to August 2026) with a 2.3-year reporting hiatus.
  • Station Micro-Environment Mismatch: Alipur (Delhi) is a single peripheral monitoring station located on National Highway 44 intercepting inter-state transit freight. In Kanpur, records are pooled across two urban stations: an institutional suburban site (NSI Kalyanpur) and a commercial/residential site (Nehru Nagar).
  • Surge Ratio Caveat: Because baselines, years, and missing data intervals differ, seasonal multipliers (5.75x vs 3.56x) are reported side-by-side as station-specific findings, not as controlled experimental comparisons.

Airshed Characteristics

While both cities are situated within the broader Indo-Gangetic Plain, their localized monitoring settings represent different exposure environments:

1. Delhi (Alipur Gateway Corridor)

Located at 28.8°N on Delhi's northern peripheral boundary along National Highway 44, the Alipur station intercepts air masses entering the capital from Punjab and Haryana. In October and November, northwesterly winds transport post-harvest agricultural burning plumes directly across this corridor. The winter fine fraction (PM2.5 / PM10) reaches 0.67, with mean Toluene / Benzene ratios of 2.18 reflecting heavy vehicular freight and exhaust.

2. Kanpur (Central Gangetic Urban Basin)

Located approximately 430 km downstream at 26.5°N along the central Ganga valley, Kanpur's monitoring records reflect a mix of urban traffic, industrial clusters, and riverine basin topography. In winter, surface cooling reduces vertical ventilation, trapping local emissions from traffic, small boilers, and domestic combustion, generating evening particulate peaks without requiring external transboundary transport.

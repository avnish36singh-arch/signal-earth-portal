Portfolio Overview

About Signal Earth

The Earth is changing faster than most people realize. Signal Earth is an independent student research portfolio dedicated to making those changes visible through maps, public data, and open science.

01 : Origins and Purpose

Why Signal Earth Exists

Every environmental shift leaves a physical record. When winter descends on Kanpur, the change is impossible to ignore: the air thickens, the horizon shrinks to a few hundred meters, and the smell of roadside wood fires and heavy diesel exhaust lingers late into the night. Yet the public discussion around air quality often feels disconnected from the physical phenomena occurring in the atmosphere. News coverage swings between seasonal panic in November and complete silence by February. Phone apps display color-coded scores without explaining how those numbers were calculated, whether the sensor down the road was operational, or what pollutants were actually measured.

Signal Earth was founded to ground these discussions in physical evidence rather than speculation. Maintained by Avnish Singh, an undergraduate student in Civil Engineering at Harcourt Butler Technical University (HBTU) in Kanpur, this portfolio parses archived government monitoring data, satellite observations, and climate reanalysis records. It is not an institution, not a live early-warning service, and not a commercial product. It is a research notebook: a place to ask precise questions about atmospheric mechanics, write reproducible Python code, and evaluate public data with engineering discipline.

02 : Foundational Tenets

Brand Philosophy

  • Evidence before opinion.
  • Data before emotion.
  • Science before speculation.
  • Maps reveal what words often cannot.
  • Every environmental change leaves evidence: Signal Earth helps people see it.

These principles shape every investigation published on this site:

  • Evidence before opinion: We do not publish political commentary or editorial speculation. Every curve, ratio, and correlation on this portal is derived directly from archived government monitors or international scientific programs.
  • Data before emotion: Air pollution is a major public health challenge, but alarmism rarely produces sound science. We examine atmospheric datasets with the same calm objectivity one brings to a structural load test.
  • Science before speculation: Atmospheric processes follow the laws of physics. When winter PM2.5 concentrations surge to five times their monsoon levels in Kanpur, we trace the cause to nocturnal boundary layer suppression and surface radiation cooling, not casual assumptions.
  • Maps reveal what words cannot: Spatial data provides geographic context that tables obscure. Overlaying Kanpur municipal boundaries onto a 55 by 60 kilometer reanalysis grid cell immediately illustrates the difference between regional models and street-level exposure.
03 : Positioning

Evidence Presented Visually

Signal Earth transforms satellite imagery, spatial maps, official datasets, and environmental reports into clear visual stories:

  • It is not activism.
  • It is not environmental news.
  • It is scientific evidence presented visually.

Editorial Tone: Calm, scientific, thoughtful, objective, and story-driven. Never sensational, never preachy, never political, and never fear-mongering. The numbers and satellite records speak for themselves when presented with context.

04 : Standards and Limits

Scientific Standards and Limits

Honest science requires disclosing what data cannot prove just as clearly as what it establishes:

  • Statutory Rigor: All AQI calculations strictly follow the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) 2014 piecewise linear formula. We separate valid statutory days (which require three criteria pollutants, at least one particulate, and 16 hours of valid data) from single-parameter estimates.
  • Preserving Data Hiatuses: Mechanical monitors in high-particulate environments experience filter tape runouts, calibration drift, and power outages. When Delhi Alipur experienced a 731-day operational hiatus between 2019 and 2020, we documented the two-year gap rather than manufacturing synthetic figures to smooth the graph.
  • Model Reanalysis vs Direct Measurement: Datasets from NASA POWER and MERRA-2 provide valuable multi-decade records of daily skin temperature, but they represent numerical weather simulations on a coarse 55 by 60 kilometer grid box. They do not measure neighborhood heat islands or street-level microclimates, which require high-resolution thermal sensors like Landsat 8 and 9 (TIRS: 100 m native, resampled to 30 m).

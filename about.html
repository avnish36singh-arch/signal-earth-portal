Background and Scope

About Signal Earth

Signal Earth is an independent student research portfolio maintained by Avnish Singh, undergraduate student in Environmental Engineering (TODO[ME]: exact enrolled degree and year) at Harcourt Butler Technical University (HBTU), Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh, India.

What Signal Earth is and is not

Signal Earth is not an institution, research laboratory, or non-profit organization. It is not a commercial product and has no advisory board or corporate entity behind it. The data processing pipelines, analyses, and visualizations presented here are the independent work of one engineering student working with public data.

The purpose of this project is straightforward: to parse publicly available environmental records, such as ambient air quality records operated by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB), the Uttar Pradesh Pollution Control Board (UPPCB), and the Delhi Pollution Control Committee (DPCC), alongside NASA POWER model reanalysis data, using reproducible Python pipelines, and to communicate the findings with scientific transparency and stated limitations.

Standards and Limitations

All index calculations strictly follow statutory standards established by regulatory bodies:

  • CPCB National Air Quality Index (NAQI) Standard: In accordance with the 2014 CPCB protocol, a valid daily AQI calculation requires at least three criteria pollutants, of which at least one must be PM2.5 or PM10, with each parameter possessing sufficient hourly completeness (at least 16 hours in a 24-hour cycle). Sub-indices are calculated using piecewise linear interpolation across calibrated breakpoint concentrations.
  • Model Validation: Machine learning forecasting models are trained on historical records and tested strictly out-of-time (e.g. training on pre-2023 records, testing exclusively on unobserved 2023 calendar data) to evaluate prospective generalization rather than memorization.
  • Data Disclosure: Missing records and instrument outages are documented explicitly. We do not bridge long-duration multi-year hiatuses with synthetic or interpolated values.

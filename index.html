Independent Research Portfolio

Air quality and surface temperature in Kanpur and Delhi

The Earth is changing faster than most people realize, but every shift leaves a physical record. Signal Earth is an independent student portfolio examining public air-quality measurements and atmospheric datasets across northern India. Here, raw station archives and satellite observations are published with reproducible Python code, explicit data gaps, and no synthetic numbers.

02 : Core Science Concepts

Explained: Atmospheric Physics and Measurement Standards

Before reading pollution numbers, one must understand how air moves, how regulators calculate scores, and what satellite models can actually see.

02A : Atmospheric Physics

What a Temperature Inversion Is, and Why Winter Air Refuses to Clear

In northern India between November and January, nocturnal radiation chills the ground and traps emissions beneath a warm atmospheric lid. This thermal inversion prevents vertical mixing, concentrating particulate matter at breathing height until morning sunlight breaks the seal.

02B : Regulatory Science

How CPCB Computes the AQI, and Why One Pollutant Governs the Day

India's National Air Quality Index is not an average, but the single highest sub-index recorded among measured criteria pollutants. Across 1,137 official monitoring days at Delhi Alipur, fine particulate matter (PM2.5) dictated the headline score on 88.4 percent of all valid days.

Read the full essay
02C : Geospatial Methods

What Weather Reanalysis Is, and What Coarse Grid Cells Cannot See

Reanalysis systems like NASA MERRA-2 provide continuous, multi-decade atmospheric simulations but average conditions over a single 55 by 60 kilometer regional cell. This grid scale captures macro seasonal heating cycles while leaving street-level microclimates and urban heat islands unresolved until mapped with 100 m native Landsat radiometry.

Aqua MODIS True Color Observation

Post-Monsoon Haze Sheet Confined by the Central Ganga River Basin

On November 11, 2021, the Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) aboard NASA's Aqua satellite captured this true-color image of central Uttar Pradesh. A pale sheet of atmospheric aerosol blankets the alluvial plain, obscuring the ground across Kanpur, Lucknow, and surrounding districts. The northern edge of the haze bank terminates sharply against the rising terrain of the Himalayan foothills, where higher elevation keeps mountain valleys clear while trapping particulate emissions within the shallow nocturnal boundary layer of the plain.

05 : Data Provenance

Data Sources and Custodians

Every analysis on Signal Earth is built entirely on open, publicly accessible scientific repositories and government monitoring programs. No proprietary or paywalled data is utilized.

CPCB (Central Pollution Control Board)

Statutory criteria pollutant concentrations (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3) and official NAQI breakpoint standards.

Terms: Central Pollution Control Board Terms of Use / Government of India Open Data
Access Date: Verified October 2026
URL: cpcb.nic.in

OpenAQ Data Platform

Harmonized REST API v3 feeds of historical hourly and daily air quality records from Uttar Pradesh Pollution Control Board stations.

Terms: Platform API under CC BY 4.0; underlying observational data subject to originating station provider terms (UPPCB / DPCC / CPCB)
Access Date: September 2026
URL: openaq.org

Daily atmospheric reanalysis skin temperature (TS) and 2-meter air temperature (T2M) over the 0.5° x 0.625° Kanpur grid box.

07 : Scientific Honesty

Known Problems and Technical Limits

A permanent record of data gaps, spatial constraints, and methodological limits across the Signal Earth codebase.

Issue / Constraint Effect on Research Findings Status Documentation Link
Delhi Alipur 731-Day Gap (2019 to 2020) Operational refurbishment halted recordings for two full calendar years (Jan 1, 2019 to Dec 31, 2020). Exactly 1,137 out of 1,825 recorded rows meet CPCB quorum. Trends cannot claim unbroken multi-year continuity. Documented Corrections Log
Kanpur 2.3-Year Hiatus (Oct 2022 to Feb 2025) Ground monitoring data is partitioned into two discrete windows (Jul 2021 to Oct 2022 and Feb 2025 to Aug 2026). Winter analyses primarily reflect 2021 to 2022 conditions. Documented Corrections Log
NASA POWER / MERRA-2 Coarse Grid (~55 x 60 km) Skin temperature is derived from an assimilation model over a regional grid cell. It cannot resolve intra-urban microclimates, street canyons, or specific industrial pockets. Documented Methodology Limits
Particulate Ratios (PM2.5 / PM10) as Screening Only Fine-to-coarse ratios provide general qualitative indications of combustion versus mechanical dust, but cannot replace chemical mass balance (CMB) or positive matrix factorization (PMF). Documented Methodology Limits
Planned Intra-Urban Spatial Zoning (Landsat / MODIS) Accurate spatial microclimate offsets across Kanpur require cloud-masked Landsat thermal radiometry (100 m native, resampled to 30 m). Founder and Operator

Avnish Singh

Undergraduate student in Civil Engineering at Harcourt Butler Technical University (HBTU), Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh, India.

Avnish founded and operates Signal Earth to examine public environmental records and spatial data with scientific transparency. Living in Kanpur, where winter haze is an intense physical reality, he noticed that public discussions of air quality often oscillate between panic and complacency, rarely examining the actual records or the physics behind them. Signal Earth takes an engineering approach: downloading raw station archives from CPCB and OpenAQ, applying statutory calculation rules, documenting multi-year sensor hiatuses without synthetic fills, and publishing all processing notebooks openly under MIT license.

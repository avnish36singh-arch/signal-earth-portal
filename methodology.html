Analytical Framework

Methodology and Limitations

Environmental data analysis carries an obligation of procedural disclosure. This document details the mathematical definitions of our global sovereign indicators, ranking protocols, in-situ regulatory validation rules, sensor hiatuses, and modeling constraints.

01 : Sovereign Indicator Formulations

Global Environmental Indicators and Mathematical Definitions

Every country dossier and ranking table on Signal Earth evaluates up to eight core sovereign indicators. All metrics are mapped to ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 entities:

Indicator ID & Unit Anchor Year Computation Protocol Primary Custodian
Annual Mean PM2.5 pm25 (µg/m³) 2021 Population-weighted ground exposure estimate combining satellite aerosol optical depth (AOD), chemical transport modeling, and ground monitoring. Benchmarked to WHO guideline (5.0 µg/m³) as Value / 5.0. WHO / IHME GBD
Yale Environmental Performance Index epi_score (score 0-100) 2026 Composite index quantifying environmental health and ecosystem vitality. Cited as an external benchmark; component weightings subject to academic debate per Yale technical appendix. Yale CIESIN
CO2 per Capita co2_per_capita (t CO2/person) 2023 Territorial fossil fuel emissions divided by United Nations medium-variant annual sovereign population: Total_CO2 / Population. Global Carbon Project
Total Territorial CO2 total_co2 (Mt CO2/yr) 2023 Total annual territorial fossil carbon dioxide emissions from energy, industrial processes, and cement manufacturing. Excludes international aviation and shipping bunkers. Global Carbon Project
Global CO2 Share share_global_co2 (%) 2023 Territorial emissions expressed as percentage share of global fossil carbon emissions: (Total_CO2 / Global_Total_CO2) * 100. Global Carbon Project
Renewable Generation Share renewable_elec_share (%) 2023 Gross electricity generated from hydro, wind, solar, bioenergy, and geothermal sources divided by total domestic gross electricity generation: (Elec_Renewables / Elec_Total) * 100. Ember Climate
Forest Area Share forest_share (%) 2020 Total land area under canopy density exceeding 10% with trees reaching minimum height of 5 meters, divided by total sovereign land area (excluding inland waters): (Forest_Area / Land_Area) * 100. FAO FRA

02 : Ranking Protocol & Staleness

Competition Ranking Algorithm and Null Data Rules

Global sovereign rankings on Signal Earth follow standard competition ranking ("1224" ranking):

  • Directionality: Indicators where higher values represent larger environmental footprint (such as PM2.5, CO2 per capita, total CO2, and temperature anomaly) are ranked in descending order (Rank 1 = highest recorded value). Indicators where higher values represent environmental transition or resource resilience (renewable generation share, forest share, and forest net gain) are ranked in descending order (Rank 1 = highest renewable share or greatest forest expansion).
  • Ties and Gaps: Countries with identical values receive the same rank. The subsequent country is assigned a rank equal to the previous rank plus the count of tied nations (for example, if two nations tie at Rank 4, both receive Rank 4, and the next nation receives Rank 6).
  • Distribution Percentile: Computed relative to the total cohort of nations reporting valid data for that indicator: Percentile = ((total_ranked - rank) / (total_ranked - 1)) * 100.
  • Strict Null Handling: If a country lacks reported data for an indicator, it is designated as Unranked and assigned null. Null data is never replaced with zero, never estimated with synthetic regional averages, and never forward-interpolated across multi-year reporting voids.
  • Year Staleness Rule: In the global rankings table, when a country's reported year differs by more than one calendar year from the indicator's anchor year, the table appends a visible warning badge: [YYYY *]. A 2019 report is never presented beside a 2023 report without an explicit staleness flag.
03 : Regulatory Monitoring

In-Situ Regulatory Air Quality Monitoring (Kanpur and Delhi)

In addition to population-weighted satellite exposure modeling, Signal Earth maintains an observational annex of regulatory Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS) operated under Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Board guidelines in northern India:

CPCB NAQI Mathematical Formulation

Under statutory CPCB protocols (October 2014), the National Air Quality Index is computed using a linear piecewise sub-index formulation for each criteria pollutant p with 24-hour average concentration Cp:

Ip = [ (IHI - ILO) / (BHI - BLO) ] * (Cp - BLO) + ILO

The composite daily AQI represents the maximum sub-index across all monitored parameters: AQI = max(IPM2.5, IPM10, INO2, ISO2, ICO, IO3, INH3, IPb). The parameter achieving the maximum sub-index is designated as the dominant pollutant.

Statutory Validity Criteria

  1. Criteria Pollutant Quorum: At least three criteria pollutants must have valid daily sub-indices.
  2. Particulate Presence: At least one valid pollutant must be particulate matter (PM2.5 or PM10).
  3. Temporal Completeness: Each pollutant requires a minimum of 16 valid hourly readings within the 24-hour cycle (at least 66.7% data completeness).
Verification in Our In-Situ Pipeline

At Delhi Alipur (Site 204), across 1,825 total calendar rows, exactly 1,137 days satisfy full statutory criteria (62.3%), while 688 days represent instrument dropouts or incomplete cycles. Particulate matter drove the official score on 95.3% of valid days (1,083 out of 1,137 days: PM2.5 on 51.8%, PM10 on 43.4%, CO on 4.4%, and Ozone on 0.4%).

Seasonal Winter Multiplier Definitions

Seasonal particulate surge multipliers reflect distinct meteorological and behavioral comparison windows:

  • Kanpur Dec-Feb vs Jun-Sep (2.40x): Compares standard meteorological winter (mean 114.7 µg/m³) to full monsoon (mean 47.8 µg/m³).
  • Kanpur Nov-Jan vs Jul-Sep (3.56x): Compares peak inversion and biomass burning months (mean 150.3 µg/m³) to core monsoon washout months (mean 42.2 µg/m³).
  • Kanpur Single-Month Extreme (4.76x): Compares peak November mean (188.4 µg/m³) to cleanest August mean (39.6 µg/m³).
  • Delhi Alipur Multipliers: Meteorological winter vs full monsoon is 4.25x (166.6 µg/m³ vs 39.2 µg/m³); peak inversion window vs core monsoon is 5.75x (197.5 µg/m³ vs 34.3 µg/m³).

Operational Hiatus Preservation

Monitoring equipment in highly polluted airsheds experiences mechanical downtime, optical recalibration, and operational refits:

  • Delhi Alipur: An operational hiatus occurred from January 1, 2019 to December 31, 2020 (731 consecutive days) during station refitting.
  • Kanpur: A hiatus occurred from October 17, 2022 to February 17, 2025 (855 consecutive days) between observation campaigns. Data across NSI Kalyanpur (815 station-days) and Nehru Nagar (813 station-days) is evaluated strictly within active operational windows.
04 : Remote Sensing & Thermal Physics

Satellite Thermal Radiometry (Landsat) vs Atmospheric Reanalysis (MERRA-2)

A fundamental distinction exists between high-resolution satellite radiometry and coarse atmospheric reanalysis:

  • USGS Landsat 8/9 TIRS-2 (30m): Directly measures top-of-atmosphere thermal infrared radiance (Band 10: 10.60-11.19 µm) at 100 m native resolution (resampled to 30 m). Inverted via Planck's radiation law, it resolves urban microclimate heterogeneity, showing an urban-core minus rural-baseline thermal delta of 1.0 to 2.3 K during cloud-free winter overpasses.
  • NASA POWER GMAO MERRA-2 (~55 x 60 km): Computes skin temperature via a thermodynamic surface energy balance parameter inside an atmospheric general circulation model. A single grid cell covers approximately 3,300 km², averaging entire metropolitan regions with rural hinterlands and muting intra-urban microclimates.
  • Temporal Nature: Landsat captures instantaneous 10:30 AM local solar time snapshots every 8 to 16 days strictly during cloudless conditions. MERRA-2 provides an unbroken daily time series across 7 consecutive years (2,556 daily records).

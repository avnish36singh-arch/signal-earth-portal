Analytical Integrity

Methodology & Limitations

Environmental data analysis carries an ethical obligation of full procedural disclosure. This page documents the mathematical definitions, statutory validity thresholds, sensor hiatuses, and modeling constraints governing every investigation published on Signal Earth.

01 · Regulatory Formula

CPCB National Air Quality Index (NAQI) Mathematical Formulation

The National Air Quality Index developed by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB, October 2014) utilizes a linear piecewise sub-index formulation. For any pollutant parameter p with observed 24-hour average concentration Cp, the sub-index Ip is computed as:

Ip = [ (IHI - ILO) / (BHI - BLO) ] × (Cp - BLO) + ILO

Where BHI and BLO represent the upper and lower breakpoint concentrations for the category containing Cp, and IHI and ILO represent the corresponding sub-index scale bounds. The composite daily AQI is determined by the maximum sub-index across all monitored criteria pollutants:

AQI = max(IPM2.5, IPM10, INO2, ISO2, ICO, IO3, INH3, IPb)

The pollutant responsible for max(Ip) is designated as the Dominant Pollutant for that monitoring day.

02 · Statutory Compliance

The Official vs. Indicative Validity Rule

Under statutory CPCB guidelines, a daily AQI value is legally and scientifically valid only when all of the following conditions are satisfied:

  1. Criteria Pollutant Quorum: At least three criteria pollutants must possess valid daily sub-indices.
  2. Particulate Presence: At least one of the validated pollutants must be either PM2.5 or PM10.
  3. Temporal Completeness: Each pollutant parameter must have a minimum of 16 valid hourly readings within the 24-hour observation cycle (≥ 66.7% data completeness).

Analytical Caveat & Audit Finding

Many commercial air quality dashboards report an "AQI" whenever any single parameter (e.g. only PM2.5) is active. In our Delhi/Alipur pipeline, of 1,825 total calendar rows, 1,137 days satisfy the full statutory criteria (62.3%), while 688 days are partial or missing. Signal Earth partitions these into two explicit fields: AQI_Official (true regulatory days) and AQI_Indicative (single-parameter estimates). We never report an indicative value as official CPCB telemetry.

03 · Telemetry Hiatuses

Sensor Gaps & Data Discontinuities

Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS) are mechanical instruments operating in extreme particulate environments. They suffer laser scattering cell fouling, beta-attenuation tape depletion, optical recalibration, and statutory relocations.

  • Delhi / Alipur (CPCB/DPCC): A major telemetry hiatus occurred from January 20, 2019 to January 22, 2021 (732 consecutive days) where all criteria pollutants went unrecorded due to station refurbishment and multi-gas analyzer recalibration. Rather than synthesizing or interpolating across a two-year void, our pipeline treats this period as a documented data hiatus.
  • Kanpur / Kalyanpur & Nehru Nagar (UPPCB/CPCB): Telemetry ingested via the OpenAQ v3 API spans July 2021 through October 2022, followed by an API stream hiatus until late 2025. Data from these stations is analyzed strictly within its verified observation windows.

Signal Earth Principle: We do not impute, synthesize, or forward-fill long-duration instrument dropouts. Data gaps are reported as missing observations on all timelines and tables.

04 · Machine Learning Evaluation

Forecasting Interpolation & Out-of-Time Testing

For time-series predictive modeling (such as our 1-day-ahead AQI Random Forest and Gradient Boosting models), continuous lag features require unbroken sequences. To maintain physical reality without hallucinating trends:

  • 2-Day Interpolation Threshold: Linear interpolation is permitted strictly for gaps of ≤ 2 consecutive days (accounting for 94.7% of genuine operational sensor dropouts). Any gap ≥ 3 days resets the lag window.
  • Strict Out-of-Time Split: Standard random k-fold cross-validation is physically invalid for atmospheric time series because it leaks future meteorological state into past predictions. Our model was trained exclusively on observations prior to 2023 and evaluated strictly on the unobserved 2023 calendar year (Test MAE: 29.8 AQI points; R² = 0.82).
05 · Source Apportionment Boundaries

Ratio-Based Source Diagnostics vs. Receptor Modeling

Our investigations utilize stoichiometric and aerodynamic ratio metrics—specifically PM2.5 / PM10, NO2 / CO, and Toluene / Benzene—to characterize dominant emission regimes:

Diagnostic Ratio Observed Threshold Physical / Chemical Indication
PM2.5 / PM10 > 0.65 Post-Monsoon / Winter Combustion dominance (biomass burning, vehicular exhaust, secondary aerosols)
PM2.5 / PM10 < 0.40 Pre-Monsoon / Summer Mechanical crustal dust dominance (resuspended road silt, construction, dust storms)
Toluene / Benzene ≈ 1.5 – 2.2 Urban Corridors Fresh tailpipe vehicular combustion emissions
Toluene / Benzene > 3.0 Industrial Belts Industrial solvent use, printing, chemical processing facilities

Scientific Distinction

These ratios represent diagnostic screening indicators, not receptor models. Full source apportionment requires chemical speciation filters (carbonaceous aerosols, elemental ions, and trace metals) coupled with EPA Positive Matrix Factorization (PMF) or Chemical Mass Balance (CMB) modeling. We state this explicitly to distinguish ratio diagnostics from chemical mass receptor analysis.

06 · Earth Observation

Satellite Land Surface Temperature (LST) & Reanalysis

Investigation 03 evaluates thermal dynamics across Kanpur using NASA POWER (Prediction of Worldwide Energy Resources) daily meteorological reanalysis, derived from the MERRA-2 assimilation model at 0.5° × 0.625° resolution, integrated with local vector GIS modeling in QGIS:

  • Radiant Skin Temperature (Tskin): Derived from thermal infrared radiance emitted by the earth's surface layer. Unlike 2-meter ambient air temperature (Tair), skin temperature responds instantaneously to solar absorption and nocturnal radiative heat loss.
  • Thermal Gradient (ΔT = Tskin - Tair): A negative thermal gradient (Tskin < Tair) indicates intense nocturnal ground cooling, which induces thermal inversion and caps atmospheric ventilation.
  • Spatial Microclimate Buffers: Urban Heat Island (UHI) offsets and riverine cooling zones are calculated using QGIS zonal spatial buffers centered on known urban morphological zones (Central Commercial Core, Jajmau/Panki industrial corridors, IITK canopy buffer, and the Ganga floodplain).

02 · Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

Satellite Reanalysis & GIS Assets

Satellite surface skin temperature and energy balance telemetry used in Investigation 03 are retrieved from open earth observation programs:

NASA POWER Project (Prediction of Worldwide Energy Resources)

Daily reanalysis parameters derived from the NASA Modern-Era Retrospective analysis for Research and Applications, Version 2 (MERRA-2) assimilation model at 0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude resolution. Parameters include Skin Temperature (TS), 2-Meter Air Temperature (T2M), Relative Humidity (RH2M), Surface Wind Speed (WS2M), and All Sky Solar Insolation (ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN).

NASA POWER API v2.0.0 · Bounding Point: 26.4499°N, 80.3319°E · Records: 2,556 daily observations (2017–2023)

QGIS Vector GIS Spatial Modeling Layers

Geographic thermal microclimate zones for Kanpur were modeled using open-source QGIS 3.x with EPSG:4326 (WGS 84) coordinate reference system, delineating the Central Urban Core, Industrial Corridors (Jajmau, Panki), the Ganga Riverine Wetland Buffer, and Academic Vegetation Canopies. Vector GeoJSON layers are hosted directly in the Signal Earth open repository.

Layers: outputs/lst/qgis/kanpur_thermal_zones.geojson & kanpur_cpcb_stations.geojson
03 · Statutory & Academic Literature

Regulatory Frameworks & Citations

  1. Central Pollution Control Board (2014): National Air Quality Index (NAQI). Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), Government of India, New Delhi. Document outlining the sub-index piecewise linear interpolation algorithm, health categories, and 3-pollutant validity criteria.
  2. Central Pollution Control Board (2009): National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS). The Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part III, Section 4. Statutory notifications setting 24-hour permissible limits for PM2.5 (60 μg/m³) and PM10 (100 μg/m³).
  3. World Health Organization (2021): WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines: Particulate Matter (PM2.5 and PM10), Ozone, Nitrogen Dioxide, Sulfur Dioxide and Carbon Monoxide. World Health Organization, Geneva. ISBN 978-92-4-003422-8.
  4. OpenAQ Community (2024): OpenAQ Global Air Quality Data Platform. OpenAQ Inc., Washington, D.C. Accessible at openaq.org. Data ingested under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).
  5. Guttikunda, S. K., & Goel, R. (2013): Health impacts of particulate pollution in a megacity—Delhi, India. Atmospheric Environment, 70, 488–498.
  6. Beig, G., et al. (2020): SAFAR: System of Air Quality and Weather Forecasting and Research. Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES), Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM), Pune.

Brief 04 · Spaceborne Thermal Infrared Radiometry

How much warmer is Kanpur's built core than surrounding fields?

By Avnish Singh, Civil Engineering undergraduate, HBTU Kanpur October 2026 Layer 1: 3 min read

The Short Answer

In clear-sky satellite thermal observations on January 15, 2022, Kanpur's dense urban core had a radiometric skin temperature +3.60 K higher than the surrounding rural agricultural periphery. High-resolution spaceborne radiometry identifies intra-urban microclimate variance that regional weather models cannot resolve.

The Evidence

Figure 1: Spatial Scale Dissonance: MERRA-2 Grid vs 30m Landsat Thermal Radiometry
Data Source: USGS Landsat 8/9 C2 L2ST & NASA POWER MERRA-2 | Scene: Jan 15, 2022
Reading the chart: Thermal infrared observations from USGS Landsat 8 (TIRS) and Landsat 9 (TIRS-2) at 100-meter native resolution (resampled by USGS to 30 meters) were mapped across concentric zones. On January 15, the core averaged 18.40°C compared to 14.80°C in the rural periphery (+3.60 K). Within the identical 55 × 60 km MERRA-2 cell (which averages Kanpur to 16.20°C), Landsat radiometry records an internal spread between extreme pixels from 13.8°C along the Ganga river to 20.9°C in dense built masonry.

What the Data Shows, in Plain Terms

Dense masonry, bitumen roads, and commercial rooftops absorb incoming solar radiation and possess high thermal inertia, while sparse vegetation (<8%) provides almost no evaporative latent cooling. As a result, the built core remains several degrees warmer than surrounding irrigated alluvium.

Simultaneously, the wide Ganga river corridor on Kanpur's northern boundary provides an active riparian thermal sink, remaining cooler than surrounding urban neighborhoods during daylight satellite overpasses. High-resolution radiometry demonstrates that urban surface temperature is intensely heterogeneous rather than uniform.

What the Data Cannot Show

These figures reflect snapshot passes at approximately 10:30 AM local solar time across two winter scenes (January 15 and February 16, 2022). They describe an observational snapshot rather than an unbroken seasonal climate. Furthermore, spaceborne radiometers measure radiative skin temperature (the physical temperature of roofs, roads, and soil), which fluctuates much more dramatically than the 2-meter ambient air temperature experienced by humans.

Why It Matters

Coarse regional climate models smooth away neighborhood-scale heat exposure for urban residents (2.77 million in Kanpur's municipal corporation, Census 2011). Resolving real microclimate hot-spots and vegetative cooling corridors provides actionable engineering evidence for targeted cool-roof deployment, urban tree canopy restoration, and local heat mitigation planning.

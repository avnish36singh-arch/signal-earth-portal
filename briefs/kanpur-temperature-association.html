Brief 03 · Atmospheric Thermal Association

Does colder weather actually drive higher air pollution in Kanpur?

By Avnish Singh, Civil Engineering undergraduate, HBTU Kanpur October 2026 Layer 1: 3 min read

The Short Answer

In concurrent observations between July 2021 and October 2022, daily surface skin temperature showed a moderate negative statistical correlation with ground-level PM2.5 (Pearson r = -0.455). However, this association reflects shared seasonal cycles across northern India rather than direct local causation.

The Evidence

Figure 1: Concurrent Trajectory: MERRA-2 Skin Temperature vs Ground PM2.5
Data Source: NASA POWER MERRA-2 & UPPCB CAAQMS | Overlap Period: Jul 9, 2021 to Oct 16, 2022 | n = 308 pairs
Reading the chart: Daily model reanalysis surface skin temperature over Kanpur was merged against ground-station particulate measurements across the 308-day concurrent monitoring window. As surface skin temperature falls from summer peaks near 40°C to winter lows below 10°C, particulate loadings surge past 150 µg/m³. Statistical evaluation yields Pearson r = -0.455 (p < 0.001).

What the Data Shows, in Plain Terms

During winter across the Indo-Gangetic Plain, strong surface radiative cooling chills the ground after sunset, creating stable thermal stratification that suppresses vertical convective mixing. Simultaneously, regional winds drop below 1.5 m/s, and seasonal biomass combustion accelerates.

Because these atmospheric and human factors synchronize each winter, ground PM2.5 concentrations consistently peak during the exact months when surface temperatures reach their minimum. Conversely, pre-monsoon summer solar heating lifts the mixing layer, and monsoon rains wash out aerosols, coinciding with annual temperature peaks. The correlation describes this coordinated annual cycle.

What the Data Cannot Show

Correlation does not establish physical causation. Because daily meteorological time series exhibit strong serial autocorrelation, the 308 observation pairs cannot be treated as independent statistical trials. Furthermore, NASA POWER provides a single daily mean temperature across a coarse 55 by 60 km grid box, which cannot measure the vertical temperature gradient (dT/dz) or directly detect the physical height of the nocturnal inversion lid.

Why It Matters

Attributing winter pollution spikes solely to low temperature risks treating severe smog as an uncontrollable act of nature. While winter meteorology severely reduces the atmosphere's dilution capacity, it is anthropogenic combustion that supplies the particulate load. Low temperature closes the room; human emissions fill it with smoke.
